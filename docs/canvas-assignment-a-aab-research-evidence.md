# Assignment A: AAB Research Evidence Brief or Evidence-Card Review

## Purpose

Convert one peer-reviewed AI-literacy or AI-education study into cautious, traceable evidence. Your work may become a **candidate contribution** to the AI Accreditation Board (AAB) evidence workflow, but it is not automatically an AAB record and is not an endorsement.

This assignment gives you practice with research discovery, study-design reasoning, claim verification, evidence-card fields, and reproducible technical writing.

## Choose one pathway

### Pathway 1: New evidence brief

Find a relevant peer-reviewed paper that is not already represented adequately in the AAB public evidence registry. Produce a proposed evidence brief and candidate field map.

### Pathway 2: Evidence-card review

Choose an existing public AAB case record, obtain its primary paper, and audit the record against the paper. Identify supported fields, reviewer interpretations, missing evidence, and any claims that require revision.

## Required workflow

1. Frame a bounded evidence question using:
   - population;
   - intervention, activity, or AI system;
   - setting or context;
   - outcome;
   - study type.
2. Search scholarly sources and record the database, complete query, filters, search date, and inclusion decision.
3. Verify bibliographic identity using the title, authors, venue, year, DOI or stable URL, and accessible paper.
4. Read beyond the abstract. Extract participants, design, comparison, measures, analysis, uncertainty, findings, limitations, and transfer conditions.
5. Build a claim-to-source matrix with at least six material claims. Give a page, section, table, or figure location for each supported claim.
6. Classify each statement as:
   - author report;
   - reviewer inference;
   - missing or unclear;
   - unsupported by the study design or source.
7. Map applicable AAB candidate fields, including learner profile, setting, educational intent, intervention, outcomes, challenges, safeguards, evidence type, transferability, and extraction confidence.
8. Give your work to a second reader. The reader must be able to recover every material claim from the cited location without your verbal help.
9. Recommend one evidence disposition:
   - **Accept** as a candidate contribution for verification;
   - **Revise** with exact repair requests;
   - **Hold** for missing-source or field verification;
   - **Reject** as research evidence while optionally retaining it as a search lead.

## Deliverables

- 800–1,200 word evidence brief or evidence-card review.
- Stable primary-source citation and accessible paper link or copy.
- Search record with database, complete query, filters, date, and screening decision.
- Claim-to-source matrix with at least six material claims.
- Completed AAB candidate field map.
- Limitations, uncertainty, missing-information, and transferability section.
- 150-word editorial note explaining what AAB could use and what still requires verification.
- Second-reader trace check with the reader’s initials and the revisions you made.

## Submission checklist

- [ ] I used the primary study, not only an abstract, news story, provider page, or AI-generated summary.
- [ ] I verified the paper’s bibliographic identity.
- [ ] Every material claim has a recoverable source location or is explicitly marked missing/unsupported.
- [ ] I separated author reports from my own interpretations.
- [ ] My causal language matches the study design.
- [ ] I reported limitations and uncertainty.
- [ ] I used nonendorsement language.
- [ ] A second reader completed the trace check.
- [ ] I proposed one evidence disposition and justified it.

## Rubric: 100 points

| Criterion | Points | Evidence of success |
|---|---:|---|
| Source relevance and bibliographic verification | 15 | The source answers the bounded question; identity and access are verified. |
| Study-design and evidence reasoning | 20 | The review accurately connects design, measures, analysis, uncertainty, and allowable claims. |
| Claim-to-source trace accuracy | 25 | At least six material claims are recoverable and correctly classified. |
| AAB field mapping and transferability | 15 | Candidate fields distinguish reported, inferred, and missing information and state transfer conditions. |
| Limitations, uncertainty, and nonendorsement | 15 | The review avoids overclaiming and names meaningful evidence boundaries. |
| Clarity, citations, and reproducibility | 10 | Another reader can repeat the search and recover the judgments. |

## Publication and grading boundary

Your grade evaluates the course submission against this rubric. It does **not** depend on public publication.

After grading, you may allow the instructor to route a copy to an instructor/AAB review queue. Publication, if any, requires independent source verification, privacy review, unsupported-claim correction, and an authorized editorial decision. AAB may accept, revise, hold, or reject the candidate contribution. A public registry record describes evidence; it does not endorse a tool, provider, curriculum, or result.
