# Optional Project 4: AI Evidence and Public Communication Contribution

## Purpose

Create one technically careful contribution that could help a public AI-literacy evidence workflow. This optional, zero-point project replaces the former Transformer and LLM Verification Mini-Lab slot; it does not add another post-midterm project.

Your course submission is graded only for completion feedback and is never automatically sent to or published by AAB. You do not need an AAB account.

## Choose one pathway

### 1. Audit an existing evidence record

Select one public AAB case, framework, assessment, policy, pilot, or research record. Compare at least six material fields or claims with the primary source. Classify each as author report, reviewer inference, missing/unclear, or unsupported, then propose exact revisions.

### 2. Propose a source-traced evidence candidate

Locate one credible AI-literacy or AI-education source not adequately represented in the public registry. Prepare a candidate record with bibliographic verification, study-design limits, a six-claim trace matrix, applicable structured fields, missing information, and a recommended accept/revise/hold/reject disposition.

### 3. Build a public AI explainer or demonstration

Translate one CPSC 481 concept for a precisely defined nontechnical audience. Preserve the causal mechanism, state the boundary of every analogy, include a meaning-matched visual or interaction, gather observable teach-back evidence, and revise the artifact from that evidence.

### 4. Conduct an accessibility or usability review

Evaluate one public AAB record or registry workflow for keyboard access, headings, link purpose, alternative text, contrast, reading order, responsive use, and plain-language comprehension. Document reproducible checks, prioritized findings, and a repair mock-up or exact remediation specification.

### 5. Create a technical visualization or validation aid

Build a small tool, diagram, schema check, claim-to-source template, comparison interface, or validation script that makes evidence easier to inspect. Include the problem statement, intended user, runnable artifact, test cases, failure boundaries, and a short use guide.

## Shared evidence requirements

Every pathway must include:

1. a one-paragraph contribution claim and intended user;
2. the primary sources or standards used, with stable links;
3. a trace showing how at least six consequential design or content decisions follow from evidence;
4. limitations, uncertainty, missing information, and excluded uses;
5. an accessibility, privacy, and safety check appropriate to the artifact;
6. a reproducibility package: files, run or review instructions, and version/date information;
7. a 200–300 word editorial note stating what could be useful publicly and what still requires independent review.

## Deliverables

- Main artifact in an appropriate format: report, annotated record, explainer, review, visualization, or runnable tool.
- Six-entry minimum evidence/decision trace.
- Source list with stable primary-source locations.
- Reproducibility or facilitation guide.
- Limitations and risk statement.
- Editorial note and the publication-choice form below.

## Completion feedback guide

Because this is a zero-point optional project omitted from the final-grade calculation, instructor comments will identify whether the completion requirements are met without altering the course grade. A complete submission demonstrates all five qualities:

| Quality | Completion evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Technical fidelity | Central claims, mechanisms, calculations, or checks are accurate and bounded. |
| Traceability | A reviewer can recover at least six material decisions from the cited evidence. |
| Usefulness | The artifact serves a named user and solves a concrete evidence or communication problem. |
| Responsible design | Accessibility, privacy, safety, uncertainty, and excluded uses are addressed. |
| Reproducibility | Another person can inspect, run, facilitate, or repeat the work without oral rescue. |

## Publication and consent boundary

Course completion and any external contribution are separate decisions.

- Your instructor evaluates the course submission first.
- Your grade and feedback do not depend on AAB acceptance, publication, or editorial preference.
- No artifact is routed to AAB without a separate, affirmative permission choice after grading.
- You may decline external sharing and still receive identical course treatment.
- If you opt in, you choose whether your name, a pseudonym, or no public attribution accompanies the candidate.
- External review may accept, revise, hold, or reject the candidate. It may require source checking, accessibility/privacy review, unsupported-claim correction, licensing confirmation, and an authorized editorial decision.
- You retain the rights you already hold in your work; permission to review or publish a copy is not a transfer of ownership.

### Post-grading publication choice

Select one only after course feedback is complete:

- [ ] Keep my work within the course.
- [ ] Permit the instructor to send a private candidate copy for AAB review; do not publish without contacting me again.
- [ ] Permit review and possible publication under the attribution choice I specify, subject to a separate final confirmation.
