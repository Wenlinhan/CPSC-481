# Meeting 16 midterm examination blueprint

This is an instructor-facing blueprint for a 75-minute Canvas assessment. It
preserves the assessment style of the Spring 2020 course while aligning the
content with the redesigned Meetings 1-15. It intentionally does not publish
the historical solution key or secure new exam items in the course site.

## Historical assessment evidence

The Spring 2020 midterm used 100 points and three major sections:

- 40 points of short explanations and problem formulation;
- 40 points of search tracing, including BFS, DFS, best-first search, A,
  admissibility, consistency, OPEN, and CLOSED;
- 20 points of minimax and alpha-beta pruning.

The historical study guide covered intelligence, definitions of AI, agents and
environments, AI fields, the Turing test, state-space search, BFS, DFS,
backtracking, heuristics, hill climbing, simulated annealing, best-first
search, A*, minimax, alpha-beta pruning, and search evaluation. The review
handout asked groups to identify three durable knowledge points and three
questions for each major topic.

## Recommended Canvas structure

1. **Readiness and policy acknowledgment - 0 points**
   - time limit, attempt policy, allowed materials, technical support, and
     accommodation statement;
   - require acknowledgment before scored items.
2. **Selected-response concepts - 20 points**
   - ten two-point items sampled from balanced banks;
   - use plausible misconception-based distractors, not vocabulary trivia.
3. **Structured calculations and traces - 45 points**
   - search trace, minimax or alpha-beta, Bayes or independence, and one
     Bellman or Q-learning calculation;
   - award method credit through separate response fields.
4. **Modeling and explanation - 20 points**
   - one problem formulation and one compare-or-explain response;
   - require assumptions and a concise justification.
5. **Evidence and failure analysis - 15 points**
   - one data, metric, reward, or state-representation case;
   - require a diagnosis, supporting evidence, and one corrective action.

## Item-bank rules

- Maintain at least three isomorphic variants for every numeric or trace item.
- Randomize values or graph labels without changing the assessed reasoning.
- Keep all variants within the same difficulty band and scoring rubric.
- Do not mix two unrelated knowledge points merely to make an item harder.
- Tag every item with meeting, knowledge point, cognitive demand, points,
  expected time, and misconception targeted.
- Keep solution keys and secure item text outside the public site repository.

## Scoring guidance

- Separate setup, procedure, result, and interpretation points.
- Give partial credit for a correct trace with one arithmetic error.
- Do not give full credit to an unexplained final answer when the learning
  outcome requires justification.
- Accept equivalent notation when semantics are correct.
- Grade evidence-analysis items on claim, evidence, reasoning, and corrective
  action.
- Review item statistics and response patterns before reusing an item.

## Accessibility and integrity

- Use text descriptions for every graph, game tree, equation image, or source
  figure.
- Avoid color-only distinctions and preserve keyboard navigation.
- Configure approved extended-time accommodations before publishing.
- Keep one question visible at a time only if backtracking is not required by
  the problem-solving design.
- If a one-page crib sheet remains permitted, state size, sidedness, and
  digital or paper rules consistently.
- Use question groups and variants as one layer of integrity; do not rely on
  surveillance as evidence of learning.

## Student-facing meeting page

The public Meeting 16 page should contain logistics, scope, skill-type practice,
time-management guidance, accessibility information, and the Canvas launch
link. It should not contain secure exam items, the historical solution key, or
answers that reveal an active item-bank variant.
